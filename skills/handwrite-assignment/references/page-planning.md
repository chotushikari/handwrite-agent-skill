# Page planning

## Goal

Distribute all required content across pages that look like plausible handwritten student work. Do not force a fixed number of topics per page.

## Procedure

1. Count the required sections, subtopics, equations, examples, diagrams, and tables.
2. Estimate visual complexity, not just word count. Equations and diagrams consume more room than prose.
3. Group related concepts together.
4. Keep a heading with its explanation and keep a diagram near the concept it explains.
5. Avoid splitting a definition or derivation across pages unless unavoidable.
6. Add a continuation marker only when it improves continuity.
7. Keep a coverage map from each source requirement to a page number.

## Practical density guidance

These are starting points, not hard limits:
- Short title + 2–3 compact sections: one page may work.
- A page with a large equation, table, or diagram should contain less prose.
- A page full of paragraph text should use fewer sections.
- If the handwriting becomes too small, split the content.
- Never omit a required topic to satisfy an arbitrary page count.

## Page plan format

| Page | Sections | Visual elements | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title and first topic | One small diagram | Header allowed |
| 2 | Next topic(s) | Equation/table | No repeated header |
| 3 | Remaining topic(s) | Summary flow | No repeated header |

## Coverage map

Maintain a mapping such as:
- Requirement 1 → Page 1, section 1
- Requirement 2 → Page 1, section 2
- Requirement 3 → Page 2, equation and explanation

At the end, verify every requirement has a page mapping and appears in the rendered output.
