# Handwriting style guide

## Target

Ordinary student handwriting from a real notebook—not a handwriting font, not calligraphy, not a polished infographic.

## Controlled irregularity

Use visible but moderate variation:
- letter height and width vary slightly;
- some letters lean a little more than others;
- cursive joins are imperfect but readable;
- word spacing is inconsistent in a natural way;
- baselines drift subtly;
- line spacing varies modestly;
- bullets and numbering are not mechanically aligned;
- underlines and circles are a little crooked;
- pen pressure and ink density vary slightly;
- some lines near the end of a paragraph may feel tighter.

Do not add random scribbles or intentional spelling errors. Do not make the writing illegible.

## Default balance

Aim for:
- 70% orderly readability;
- 30% natural irregularity.

Increase irregularity only when the user asks. Preserve academic legibility as the hard constraint.

## Ink and paper

- Dark or medium blue ballpoint ink.
- Blue ink for all diagrams, arrows, tables, and underlines.
- Plain white or off-white paper.
- Subtle paper grain and mild bends.
- Natural soft shadow.
- Slight phone-camera perspective, but no dramatic angle.

## Avoid

- purple, red, green, or yellow highlights by default;
- coloured diagrams or colour-coded boxes;
- identical letter shapes repeated across the page;
- perfect horizontal baselines;
- uniform word spacing;
- geometric-perfect tables;
- calligraphy, brush lettering, or decorative headings;
- extreme wrinkles, dirt, stains, or torn paper.

## Consistency across pages

Keep the same general handwriting personality, ink tone, paper appearance, and camera treatment across pages. Allow small variations in spacing, slant, and pressure so the pages do not look mechanically duplicated.

## Reference photographs

Use references only for general visual characteristics. Never copy personal or unrelated text from the reference. If the reference has a different ink colour or page format than the user requests, follow the user's explicit request.
